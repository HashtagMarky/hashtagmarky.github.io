Whilst I'm not usually a fan of novellas, Ring Shout has changed my mind. While it's a short tale, it's impact was massive in proportion to its length, somehow introducing us to characters and giving the reader all the understanding necessary to become desperately attached. The culture throughout this book is amazing, and leaves a strong impression of a depth and richness the individuals in this book have, and is a great representation of the Gullah Geechee people.

The book starts following a young woman called Maryse, she's rageful yet determined and that makes her a dangerous warrior. She, and her crew, are not just resistance fighters, but literal demon hunters in the terrifying environment of Macon. And while I'm not always a fan of the 'chosen one' trope, the subtlety and reasoning behind it made for an amazing story throughout.

The other characters such as Sadie and Chef complimented Maryse and the story beautifully. They provided everything from literal ballistic and explosive support to comedic relief between the horrors of the Ku Kluxes. The depersonification of Klansmen into literal all consuming demons from hell made for a gripping tale, that usually wouldn't be my cup of tea, but one that I loved from start to finish. Ring Shout was an absolute treat to experience, one that anybody could tell that P. Djèlí Clark put their soul into.
